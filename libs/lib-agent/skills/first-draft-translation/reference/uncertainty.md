# Marking uncertainty

This is the most important discipline of the entire workflow.

For every passage where the interpretation is not secure:

- **Translate your best reading in the body text.**
- **Do not mark the uncertainty inline in the body.** Attach a note reference at
  the point of difficulty and put the marker at the *start of the note*: `[?]`
  for minor uncertainty, `[??]` where the editor must adjudicate. The body text
  should read as clean, continuous prose or verse, with only the note reference
  showing that something needs attention. All of the substance — what is
  unclear, the alternatives, your reasoning — lives in the note.
- **Explain the difficulty in the note**: the Tibetan phrase at issue, the
  plausible alternatives, and why you chose the reading you did.
- **Never resolve a genuine ambiguity by choosing the smoothest English.** A
  flagged awkwardness is worth more to the editor than a hidden guess — but the
  flag belongs in the note, not as inline clutter in the sentence.

Classify each flagged item where possible:

| | |
|---|---|
| (a) | grammatical ambiguity in the Tibetan |
| (b) | uncertain term of art |
| (c) | suspected corruption |
| (d) | doctrinal or interpretive question |
| (e) | unclear referent |
| (f) | alignment boundary uncertain |

## Alignment uncertainty is uncertainty too

The same discipline applies to alignment, not only to meaning. If
`tibetan-english-passage-alignment` reports **moderate** or **low** confidence
for a passage pair's boundary, give that passage a note reference as well,
classify it as (f), and explain why the boundary is unclear — a connective or
honorific frame phrase that does not map 1:1, a condensed formula, and so on.

## Not this

- Do not leave an uncertainty marker (`[?]` / `[??]`) inline in the running
  translation body. It belongs at the start of the corresponding footnote.
- Do not invent citations, attestations, or lexical authorities in notes.
