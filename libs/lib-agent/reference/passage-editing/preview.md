# Preview before writing

A passage edit has no undo. Show the editor what you are about to do and wait
for them to confirm it. This conversational preview **is** the dry-run gate;
there is no other one.

## What a preview has to contain

- **Counts.** How many passages change, how many annotations are created,
  changed or deleted.
- **What changed and where**, concretely enough to check — a label and the text
  around the edit, not a total.
- **What you decided not to touch, and why.** A reviewer cannot see the cases
  you rejected, and those are where a wrong rule hides.
- **What you were unsure about**, each with the reason it was doubtful and what
  acting on it would cost.
- **Anything that did not resolve** — an identifier you could not look up, a gap
  in a sequence, a passage whose shape did not match what you expected.

## When unsure, ask — do not skip

Silently leaving a doubtful case alone looks like the safe choice and is not. It
under-delivers the work and buries the evidence that the case exists, so nobody
learns the rule was wrong.

In a corpus edited by people over many years, an oddity is usually somebody
breaking a convention rather than a category that deserves its own rule. An
editor can tell which in a second; you cannot.

So sort candidates three ways, not two: **convert**, **ask**, **reject**.
Reserve rejection for what is definitely not the thing you are looking for.
Anything shaped like it but malformed is an ask.

## Do not resolve an ask by choosing

Having raised a case, wait for the answer. Picking the likely option and
mentioning it in passing is not a preview — the editor has no way to tell your
judgment calls from your certainties, and will read the whole preview as
certainties.

## Report what you did afterwards

After writing, say what actually landed and what did not, including anything you
skipped because the editor declined it. A preview the editor approved is not
evidence that the write matched it.
