## G. Mantras and dhāraṇīs

See IV.A above for general specifications regarding Sanskrit.

Unless there is a Sanskrit edition available, mantras and dhāraṇīs should in most cases be transcribed (using IAST transliteration) as they are in the Degé original. An exhaustive documentation of all variants is not required, but significant differences compared to other Kangyurs (and other instances of the same or a closely related text in other sections) should be noted. If the Sanskrit so yielded is problematic, editing to produce a more plausible result should only be attempted if it can be done with confidence and on the basis of attested parallels or clearly better versions in other source texts.

Capitalization should be avoided in standalone mantras and dhāraṇīs not rendered in English translation, even for the first word. When mantras, dhāraṇīs, and single syllables are mixed with English translation, they should be capitalized and punctuated according to English conventions.

Longer dhāraṇīs can be divided using the vertical line character ( | ) to represent a _daṇḍa_ or _shad_. Their placement should be a matter of careful judgment; it is usually not necessary to so represent every single _shad_ in the Tibetan, but if the dhāraṇī can reasonably be divided into phrases it will probably be easier to read. It may be particularly helpful to divide multiple repetitions of single syllables into groups. Longer dhāraṇīs should end with a double _daṇḍa_.

The phrase _tadyathā_ should be left in Sanskrit but should always be followed by a _daṇḍa_; this is a compromise between its being traditionally understood in Tibetan Buddhist practice to be part of the mantra or dhāraṇī on the one hand, and its role simply as an opening conjunction as often understood from the perspective of Sanskrit texts on the other.

If all or part of a mantra or dhāraṇī can be translated with confidence, this can be done in endnotes. The note reference number for such an endnote should be placed after the final double _daṇḍa_.
